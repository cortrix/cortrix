# Milestones in Deep Learning

Artificial neural networks date back to 1943, when Warren McCulloch and Walter
Pitts proposed a mathematical model of biological neurons. Frank Rosenblatt's
perceptron, built in 1958, was the first trainable neural network machine, but
interest collapsed after Minsky and Papert's 1969 book demonstrated the
limitations of single-layer perceptrons.

The backpropagation algorithm, popularized by Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams in
1986, enabled the training of multi-layer networks and revived the field. Yann
LeCun applied convolutional neural networks to handwritten digit recognition at
Bell Labs in 1989; his LeNet-5 architecture was later used by banks to read
checks.

The modern deep learning era began in 2012, when AlexNet, designed by Alex
Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton, won the ImageNet image
classification challenge by a dramatic margin, cutting the top-5 error rate from
26 percent to 15.3 percent. AlexNet's success relied on training with GPUs, the
ReLU activation function, and dropout regularization.

The Transformer architecture, introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You
Need" by Vaswani and colleagues at Google, replaced recurrence with self-attention
and became the foundation of modern large language models.

In 2024, Geoffrey Hinton and John Hopfield received the Nobel Prize in Physics for
their foundational discoveries enabling machine learning with artificial neural
networks, a recognition of how deeply the field has influenced science.
