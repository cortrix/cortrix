# A Brief History of the Internet

The Internet traces its origins to ARPANET, a packet-switching network funded by
the United States Department of Defense. ARPANET delivered its first message on
October 29, 1969, between computers at UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute.
The first transmission attempt famously crashed after sending only the letters
"LO" of the word "LOGIN".

The TCP/IP protocol suite, developed by Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn in the 1970s, became
the standard networking protocol of ARPANET on January 1, 1983 — a date often cited
as the birthday of the Internet. TCP/IP allowed heterogeneous networks to
interconnect, forming a true "network of networks".

The World Wide Web was invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 while working at CERN
in Switzerland. He proposed a system of hypertext documents linked across the
network, and by the end of 1990 he had implemented the first web browser, the
first web server, and the foundational technologies HTML, HTTP, and URLs.

The Mosaic browser, released in 1993 by the National Center for Supercomputing
Applications, popularized the Web with its graphical interface and inline images.
Netscape Navigator followed in 1994 and quickly dominated the early browser market.

Domain names are coordinated by ICANN, the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names
and Numbers, founded in 1998. The Domain Name System itself was designed by Paul
Mockapetris in 1983, replacing the centrally maintained HOSTS.TXT file.
